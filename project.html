Choose one mission

All four missions start from TinyStories-33M, a pretrained story-writing model. Use the public validation data to develop your method. Final staff evaluation uses held-out data that is not released.

M1 Data efficiency

Constraint
Train on at most 100 stories selected from the supplied pool of 10,000 TinyStories.
You design
Which stories to select and how to train on them.
Score
Perplexity on held-out stories. Lower is better.

M2 Fine-tuning efficiency

Constraint
A fixed pool of 50,000 TinyStories and 20 minutes of training on a Colab T4 GPU.
You design
How to fine-tune within the training budget.
Score
Perplexity on held-out stories. Lower is better.

M3 Retention

Constraint
Train 200 steps on TinyStories, then 500 steps on TinyStoriesInstruct.
You design
Training methods for both phases that learn the new task while retaining the original one.
Score
Original-task perplexity after both phases. Lower is better. New-task perplexity must also be 4.2 or lower.

M4 Steerability

Constraint
A fixed base model with no weight updates. Each steering call must take 10 seconds or less.
You design
A steer function that generates a story with three required words. Your code may not write or edit the story.
Score
Share of held-out prompts whose story contains all three words and reads fluently.

What you submit: M1–M3 require a fine-tuning algorithm and the saved trained model. M4 requires a steering function. Every team also designs and submits a benchmark. The starter code supplies the interfaces, data, and baseline metric.

How the project works

  1. Individual write-up: each student proposes an algorithm and benchmark design for the team’s mission.
  2. Group submission: discuss the proposals, select one algorithm and one benchmark, and implement them using the starter code.
  3. Group presentation: present your approach and how it performed on different benchmarks.

After Milestone 2, staff will run every team’s algorithm against every other team’s benchmark in the same mission, plus a held-out staff benchmark. Results will be posted on a leaderboard. The top-ranked team in each mission earns bonus points.

The project is worth 30% of your course grade, divided equally across the three milestones:

  • Individual proposal: 10% of your course grade, graded individually.
  • Group submission: 10% of your course grade, graded as a team.
  • Presentation: 10% of your course grade, based on the team presentation and your individual answer to a question.

We will release rubrics and grading criteria for each milestone.

Teams

  • Teams have 2 to 4 members. Submit your roster and mission choice using the team sign-up form by Monday, October 26.
  • Group contract (optional). We recommend a contract describing individual responsibilities, meeting frequency, and group deadlines. If you upload a signed contract with the sign-up form, staff will enforce it to help resolve conflicts. Without a contract, staff cannot mediate group disputes, and the group is responsible for ensuring project development goes smoothly.

  • Team issues. Report conflicts through a private Piazza post to instructors rather than in office hours, so staff have a written record.

Starter code

Each mission has a Colab notebook. All four use shared helpers from common.py (data loading, perplexity scoring, model save/load). Do not change function signatures. Staff run your submission through them without modification.

  • M1 Data efficiency: notebook
  • M2 Fine-tuning efficiency: notebook
  • M3 Retention: notebook

Download the notebook for your mission and common.py. When you run in Colab, upload common.py to the Colab workspace (the Files panel on the left) in the same session, or the notebook's imports will fail. Re-upload it if the runtime resets.

AI use policy

You may use AI tools to develop ideas, write and debug code, and create benchmarks. Benchmark data may be handwritten, selected from permitted datasets, AI-generated, or a combination. Follow the project’s data restrictions and keep benchmark data separate from training data. Your benchmark must be self-contained, with data and a scoring method that staff can run.

All training and evaluation runs used for submitted results must run in a Google Colab T4 environment within your mission’s computing limits. If your algorithm or benchmark requires accessing external AI tools or services at runtime, you will receive a zero for that component.

You may use AI to improve the visual appearance of figures and slides while accurately representing your results. You must write all report and slide text yourselves. AI may not draft, rewrite, or polish this text. Briefly disclose your AI use and cite outside sources. You are responsible for understanding and checking everything you submit. During presentations, we will ask challenging questions about your work. If AI completes the project for you without your developing that understanding, you will be unprepared to answer.
